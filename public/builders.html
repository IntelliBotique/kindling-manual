What it’s for
Building on Kindling: one of the four conformance classes in §11, or a registry, block list or agent that §11 does not name.
What it’s not for
A hosted consumer product, or a chargeable surface at the connection layer. §1.3 lists both as non-goals.

What are you building?

Pick one. The page shows the sections that bind it, each MUST quoted, and what to read. Nothing is stored, and coming back to this page starts from nothing chosen.

The sections that bind it

  • §3.1: “A Pool MUST be retrievable as a JSON document, either from a public Git repository or from a Pool API endpoint that returns the same shape.”
  • §5.1: “A Profile MUST NOT be added to a Pool without the owner’s explicit consent.”
  • §5.3: “Implementations MUST process withdrawal within 60 seconds of receipt.”
  • §9.1: “the Pool MUST enter dormant status. Pool members MUST see a notice. The well-known file MUST reflect dormancy.”
  • §10.2: “MUST list, for each discoverable Pool on the domain, its manifest_url, visibility and status”

Art. III

What the protocol leaves out

§1.3 lists “a chargeable surface at the connection layer” among the non-goals, and K.1 shows which of the site’s ten lines rest on no MUST. The Field Manual’s moats chapter is the case the protocol answers.

A builder who will sell something beside the protocol reads K.3, money beside the introduction.

  • 3.5 Moats in the AI EraFrom the Field Manual, on solo.joshwolf.net

    For a Kindling reader This chapter teaches the moat. Kindling defines no chargeable surface between two people (§1.3, a stated non-goal). The project calls Pools a commons, and its Why page says: “When you can leave without losing anyone, streaks, paywalled likes and fake urgency lose their leverage.” See K.3.
