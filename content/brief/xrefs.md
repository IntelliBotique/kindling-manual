## Pointers the existing chapters should gain (for the Kindling edition only)

1. **Ch 3.5, Moats in the AI Era.** A Kindling reader needs a line that the protocol names as a non-goal the moat this chapter teaches at the connection layer: "Pools are a commons" and the Why page's "When you can leave without losing anyone, streaks, paywalled likes and fake urgency lose their leverage." Point to K.3 and §1.3.
2. **Ch 3.6, Pricing Strategy.** Assumes the product has a price. Add: "On Kindling, the introduction has none; price the thing beside it. See K.3." Add also that 3.6's "Offer a lock-in" is a price lock on the thing sold and never applies to a Pool.
3. **Ch 3.7, Outcome, Performance and Usage Pricing.** Its success-fee and percentage models are closed to anything an introduction produces. Add a one-line pointer to K.3.
4. **Ch 4.3, Contracts.** The terms-of-service section teaches clickwrap and recording "which version each user accepted and when." A Kindling Pool's consent record is the `consent_proof` of §3.4, set by a POST on a confirmation page (§5.2). Point to K.4.
5. **Ch 4.7, Privacy and AI Regulation.** §2.5 (no photo bytes), §2.7 (noindex) and §2.3 (recording what was inferred) are worked examples of the chapter's principles. Point to K.1.
6. **Ch 4.8, Risk.** Add a pointer to K.4's invented curator risk register.
7. **Ch 7.1, Distribution Is the Scarce Asset.** For builders, the protocol's distribution story is the registry and the well-known file (§8.3 to §8.5, §10). Point to K.1.
8. **Ch 8.5, Security and Reliability.** The v0.1.1 confirmation step ("email security scanners open every link in a message," §5.2) is a clean case study. Point to K.1.
9. **Ch 10.4, Loneliness and Identity.** Leads Volume 10 in this edition. It needs no Kindling text inside it; the edition must not frame the protocol as a remedy.
